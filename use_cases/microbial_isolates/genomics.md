# Metagenome

## Background

Short sequencing reads are small fragments of DNA sequence, typically
ranging from 50 to 300 base pairs, that are generated by high-throughput
sequencing technologies like Illumina. These reads represent tiny
overlapping pieces of a much larger genome or DNA molecule.

Assembly is the computational process of reconstructing the original
longer DNA sequence by finding and aligning the overlapping regions
between these short reads, like piecing together a jigsaw puzzle where
the edges of adjacent pieces must match perfectly.

# Microbial isolate genomes for whole genome gene-content foundation model

This dataset comprises whole genome shotgun sequencing data from 11
bacterial isolates, generated on the Illumina platform as paired-end
150bp reads. These samples represent non-pathogenic bacterial isolates
and were sequenced to capture comprehensive genomic information across
each organism\'s genome. The data provides a snapshot of the genetic
diversity and gene content present across different bacterial species or
strains, making it well-suited for computational analysis of genomic
features and gene repertoires.

This example dataset serves as a representative subset of the larger
training data that will be used to develop a whole genome gene-content
foundation model. The AI model will learn to recognize patterns in
bacterial gene content, genomic architecture, and the relationships
between different genetic elements across diverse bacterial genomes. By
training on this type of data at scale, the foundation model will
develop the ability to understand and predict gene presence/absence
patterns, functional gene clusters, and genomic organization principles
that can be applied to novel bacterial genomes. The paired-end
sequencing format provides sufficient read length and coverage to
accurately assemble and annotate genes, while the multi-sample structure
allows the model to learn cross-genome patterns rather than features
specific to individual isolates.

## Data Pipeline

Here\'s a rough pipeline to transform FASTQ files into a training
dataset for a gene-content foundation model:

**Preprocessing and Quality Control**

-   Quality assessment of raw reads to identify any technical issues

-   Adapter trimming and quality filtering to remove low-quality
    sequences

-   Optional: host/contaminant DNA removal if relevant

**Genome Assembly**

-   De novo assembly of paired-end reads into contigs/scaffolds

-   Assembly quality assessment (N50, genome completeness, contamination
    checks)

**Gene Prediction and Annotation**

-   Gene calling to identify coding sequences across assembled genomes

-   Functional annotation to assign gene functions, protein families,
    and ontologies

-   Optional: non-coding RNA prediction if relevant to the model

**Feature Extraction and Standardization**

-   Extract gene-content profiles (which genes are present/absent in
    each genome)

-   Cluster or categorize genes into orthologous groups or protein
    families for consistent representation across samples

-   Generate gene embeddings or convert sequences to numerical
    representations

**Dataset Formatting**

-   Organize data into structured format suitable for model training
    (e.g., feature matrices, sequence databases, graph representations),
    maybe in HDF5 format

-   Split into training/validation sets if this expands beyond example
    data

-   Create metadata files linking samples to their genomic features

This pipeline converts raw sequencing reads into structured gene-content
information that captures what genes each bacterial genome contains,
which is the foundation for training your model to understand patterns
in bacterial genomic repertoires.
